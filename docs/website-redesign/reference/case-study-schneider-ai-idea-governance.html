    In a nutshell

    The hub's engineers originate and own their AI projects. In an ongoing engagement, we are building and advising on the intake and governance platform that lets them register ideas in under two minutes, gives the governance team one place to triage and support them, and shows leadership the whole portfolio, all inside Schneider Electric's own Microsoft 365 tenant.

    Client data protectedNames, ideas, counts and screens on this page are sanitised or changed. The workflow is real; the data is not.
    01 · The problem

    Eighty engineers. Ideas everywhere. No single view.

    The hub wanted engineers to own their AI projects. That left leadership unable to answer four basic questions. Tap one to see the gap.

    The NAM MV Innovation Hub
    About 80 engineers across seven business areas · positions and counts are sanitised
    What was missing
    • Visibility: no single place captured every AI idea, so leadership could not see what was built, on which platform, or by whom.
    • A support path: a stuck engineer had no defined way to ask for help, and nobody could spot them.
    • Sensitive ideas: some touch confidential R&D, customer or export-controlled material and needed review without slowing everyone else.
    • Ownership kept: the answer could not be a central team building tools and pushing them down.

    03 · Steps 1 and 2 · Intake

    Two minutes in. A few seconds behind the screen.

    Fill in the form like an engineer would. Follow-up questions appear only when they apply. Submit it and watch the intake flow run.

    Real form: 7 of 9 questions showing · target under 2 minutes
    AI Intake · Form to Registrywaiting for a response
      Why intake is built this way
      • Nine questions, mostly hidden: four core questions plus identity; the "other tool" and "what kind of help" questions appear only when they apply.
      • A standalone form on purpose: a list-bound form would skip the flow, losing the identity lookup, the sensitivity check, the messages and the failure alert.
      • Ownership in one variable: the governance owner is read from one setting, so it can change without rebuilding any step.
      • Never lost silently: a parallel branch alerts the governance owner if the SharePoint write fails.
      04 · Steps 3 to 5 · Triage, pipeline and Teams

      Seven stages. Drag a card, and everyone hears.

      Drag a card to another column, or tap a card and then a column. The move is validated, saved to SharePoint and announced in Teams.

      Write-back onSanitised data
      Write-back trace
        What the engineer sees in Teams
        How a move is saved and announced
        • A board that writes back: Power BI is normally read-only, so a dropped card goes to an Azure Function that rejects unknown IDs and any status outside the seven.
        • One timestamp everywhere: a Power Automate flow stamps one UTC time and saves it to SharePoint, so the card and the record always agree.
        • Teams, not email: a status-change flow posts the matching message; it ignores items at Submitted, so the intake write never fires it.
        • Honest failures: if a save fails, the board reports an error and asks the user to refresh, because a timed-out save may still have completed.
        05 · Step 6 · Sensitive data review

        Flag the few. Don't stop the many.

        Answer the sensitivity question as an engineer, then act as the governance owner. Watch who can see the record.

        The engineer answers Would it touch confidential, R&D, customer or export-controlled material?
        Who can see "Supplier Quality Insights"
        Audit trail
          How sensitive ideas are handled
          • Set by the engineer's own answer: any answer other than No, including Not sure, sets the flag. Over-flagging is the safer error.
          • Visible at once: the governance alert starts with [SENSITIVE DATA REVIEW] and the idea lands in a dedicated Sensitive Review view.
          • Locked when needed: item-level permissions restrict the record to governance owners and the assigned support contact.
          • Same register: sensitive ideas stay in the one list, so reporting and the lifecycle work the same way.

            What leadership gets
            • At-a-glance counts: total ideas, and those needing triage, needing support, in build, deployed and under sensitive review.

            A closed loop around one SharePoint list

            No separate database, no external API, no third-party SaaS. Pick a direction to trace how data moves.

            Built to be owned by the client
            • Three flows and one function: intake, status-change notification and board write-back, plus the UpdateIdeaStatus function in Schneider Electric's Azure subscription.
            • Standard licensing: intake, register and notifications run on E3/E5, with no external SaaS, no Dataverse and no Copilot Studio dependency.
            • Details that matter: column names and choice values match the form character for character, or SharePoint rejects the write.
            • Handed over in full: a technical design document and a rebuild guide with a check at every step, so the hub runs it without Paramount.
