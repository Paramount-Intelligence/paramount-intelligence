    In a nutshell

    The client's sales and delivery teams assembled every Statement of Work by hand. We built a browser-based SOW authoring and document-generation platform, orchestrated through eight n8n workflows, that saves versioned drafts, builds timelines from reusable content and 30+ business rules, preserves reviewed edits when scope changes, and renders the final Word document from the approved state.

    Client data protectedNames, scope, hours and screens on this page are sanitised or changed. The workflow is real; the data is not.
    01 · The problem

    Same scope. Different SOW. Every time.

    Timelines lived in people's heads. Give the same scope to two estimators and you got two plans. Ask them again and watch it happen.

    What was going wrong
    • Copy, paste, re-enter: reusable technical content and engagement details were copied between SOWs by hand.
    • Timelines by judgment: two people could produce different delivery plans from the same scope.
    • Fragile edits: a late scope change meant regenerating the timeline, and reviewer edits were lost.
    • Content tied to formatting: layout and content were coupled, so every change risked the other.
    02 · The approach

    Generate, review, then render. In that order.

    Automation should raise consistency without removing judgment. So the platform splits generation from rendering, and the reviewed timeline becomes the source of truth. Step through one SOW.

    The principleOnce a timeline has been reviewed, the approved version is authoritative. The final document is always rendered from it, never silently regenerated. Reopening a saved SOW loads that approved timeline as it was saved, not a fresh one.
    One engagement, start to finish
    • Enter it once: someone on the sales or delivery team fills in the company, SOW details, scope, technical answers and signers in the Form App, and saves a draft.
    • Review the plan: the platform builds the timeline from the rules; a reviewer edits tasks, adds rows and reorders them, then saves it as approved.
    • Change the scope: if scope changes after review, the platform removes only the generated rows owned by the dropped scope, adds the new scope before the closing phases, and keeps the reviewer's edits.
    • Render the document: the Word file is built from the approved timeline, using the standard or Upgrade & Training template.
    • Hand off to readiness: once the SOW is signed, its scope seeds the readiness checklist, one section per technology, to prepare the project for implementation.
    03 · The rule engine

    Pick a scope. Watch 30+ rules build the timeline.

    The real scope options from the Form App. Toggle them and the engine assembles delivery weeks, applying the codified rules you can see firing alongside.

    Rules fired

      Rule behaviour is real (BR-001 to BR-032). Technology hours are illustrative; in production they come from the Qdrant content catalog and approved overrides.

      How a timeline is assembled
      • Normalize first: scope is reduced to four canonical groups, aliases are resolved against the catalog, and duplicates removed.
      • Content from Qdrant: technology blocks come from the sow_templates collection; rules, fixed blocks and hour overrides from PostgreSQL.
      • Capacity-aware: a codified planning rule (BR-025) gives each timeline week a capacity of 40 hours; content that overflows creates continuation weeks.
      • Fixed closing phases: Requirements opens when DevOps exists; Cutover, HyperCare and Project Wrap close, sized by DevOps count.

      Removed vs keptDropping scope is deliberate, so the generated rows it owns go with it. Rows a reviewer added stay, even when they share a week with the removed scope.
      How reconciliation works
      • The backend decides the delta: added, removed and unchanged scope are calculated from previous and current scope, never trusted from the browser.
      • Rows know their owner: generated rows carry scope group, key, label and source week, so only removed scope's rows are removed.
      • Human edits survive: mixed weeks with reviewer content are preserved; new scope is inserted before the fixed closing phases.
      • Tidy finish: aggregates rebuild, zero-hour SCM rows merge into host weeks, and the sequence is renumbered.
      05 · The constrained Copilot

      Conversational, but never in control

      Type a command, or pick one. Known commands skip the model entirely. Free-form ones go to Claude as a parser, and the answer is validated before anything changes. The model never touches the database.

      ⚡
      Validated result
      { }
      Command handling is simulated on this page to show the real routing logic
      Why the AI stays boxed in
      • Deterministic first: create, open and copy lifecycle commands are recognised by code and never sent to a model.
      • A parser, not an agent: free-form text goes to an Anthropic model at temperature 0 with an allow-list of operations and a fixed output schema.
      • Validated, then routed: unsupported operations are rejected; ordinary edits stay in the browser, lifecycle actions go to the database.
      • Parameterized SQL only: every state-changing database action is built by workflow logic, never written by the model.

      • Independent failure modes: a formatting defect can't alter a correct timeline, and a timeline fix never needs a template change.

      08 · Architecture and operations
      
            Eight workflows. Three stores. One rollback away.
            
      
            Responsibilities are split across eight n8n workflows instead of one monolith. Tap a workflow to see its job, or switch to the release path every production change follows.
            
    
            
    
            
      
            
      
            
      
            
    
            
    
            Built to be recovered
              
      
            • One owner per webhook: every production path has exactly one active workflow, so routing never collides.
              • 
      
            • Change a copy first: edits happen on inactive workflow copies, tested against representative fixtures before promotion.
              • 
      
            • Back up everything: PostgreSQL, Qdrant snapshots, templates and workflow JSON exports before every deployment.
              • 
      
            • No secrets in exports: credentials are excluded from workflow exports and from all documentation.
