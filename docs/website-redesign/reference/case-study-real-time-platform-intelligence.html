    Every journey, watched live.

    A real-time platform intelligence system for a leading ride-hailing and delivery platform. It tracks millions of user journey events and shows where a journey breaks, and why, before the problem becomes a customer complaint.

    • The journey is the unit: every team watches the same customer journey, not its own separate dashboard.
    • Cause, not symptom: each break is traced to marketing, UX, supply, system or support risk.
    • While customers are active: teams act during the journey, not after the complaint.

      Leading ride-hailing and delivery platform

      In a nutshell

      The platform had no live, end-to-end view of how its ecosystem was working. Teams relied on delayed reports and separate dashboards that showed outcomes but not causes, so finding the source of a problem meant manual investigation across teams and tools. We built a real-time platform intelligence layer that treats every interaction as a signal in one live journey. It shows where a journey breaks and whether the cause is marketing, UX, supply, the system or support risk, while customers are still active. Product, operations, marketing and leadership now work from a single real-time operational truth.

      Client data protectedThe client is not named. Event names, zones, campaigns, journeys and questions in the demos are samples; no client data is shown. The system is real.
      01 · The problem

      Dashboards showed what happened, not why

      Each team had its own dashboard, and each one looked fine on its own. Pick a symptom, then look at the journey.

      ?

      Illustration · sample symptoms and journeys

      The blind spots
      • Conversion drops: caused by UI/UX friction, supply shortages or technical failures? No one could say.
      • Cancellations: behavioural, operational or system-driven?
      • Campaigns: bringing quality demand, or low-intent traffic?
      • Support issues: could they have been prevented earlier in the journey?
      • Found too late: issues surfaced only after complaints or performance had already dropped.
      • Blame cycles: without a shared view of where failures started, teams argued instead of acting together.
      02 · The principle

      One journey, watched by every team

      Every department touches the same customer journey. So the journey, not the department, became the unit of monitoring. Pick a team to see the part of the journey it watches.

      “Every department interacts with the same user journey, so the journey itself should be the unit of monitoring.”

        Success was defined as
        • Diagnosing issues while users were still active, not in the next day’s report.
        • Separating UX, operational, supply and technical failures, so the right team acts first.
        • One operational view across teams, grounded in the same real-time event stream.

          Five ways a journey breaks
          • Marketing: users land from a campaign but never move on to pricing or a ride request. That is low-intent traffic or misaligned targeting, not a product failure.
          • UI / UX: repeated navigation between screens, returning to the same page without progressing, or journeys that stall point to friction in a specific part of the app.
          • Supply: a ride request with no driver assigned inside a set window, for example two minutes, is flagged as a supply imbalance.
          • System: an event fires (for example, ride started) but the next expected event never arrives. That points to a backend failure, an API issue or an app crash.
          • Support risk: breakpoints that usually end in a complaint are caught before the customer contacts support.
