    In a nutshell

    We built and deployed a production AI assistant embedded in the operator's live consumer app. Customers verify with OTP, then get answers from their own account and the operator's approved documents, without leaving the app.

    01 · The problem

    The cost of friction

    Customers were already in the app every day, but getting help meant leaving it.

    Where does a customer go for help?
    Times the customer leaves the app
    0

    Helpline, hold queue, web portal. Every question breaks the experience.

    What was going wrong
    • A huge range of everyday queries: bundles, balance, data usage, recharge and call history, SIM details, coverage and complaints.
    • Manual, static flows: high query volume strained support operations and left customers waiting for answers.
    • Support lived outside the app: customers already in the app every day had to leave it to call a helpline or visit a web portal.
    • The need: an assistant inside the operator's own app that answers account-specific questions securely, without the customer ever leaving.
    What customers ask

    One assistant, three kinds of question

    How the assistant handles each kind
    • My account data: after OTP, the assistant reads the verified customer's own balance, usage, bundles, recharges, call history and SIM details.
    • Approved knowledge: coverage, network policy and SOP questions are answered by Amazon Kendra from the operator's approved documents, not improvised.
    • Actions the assistant completes: it registers complaints with a reference ID, initiates support requests and escalates what it can't resolve.
    • Actions the assistant guides: bundle subscriptions and usage alerts go step by step, and nothing changes until the customer confirms. The assistant never activates a package or changes a plan on its own.
    • Both kinds of answer, one chat: generic and account-specific questions are handled in the same conversation, inside the app.
    02 · The complication

    Four non-negotiables, held at the same time

    Tap each one to lock it in.

    0/4Held
    Why each one mattered
    • In-app, not a portal: it had to live inside the operator's live consumer app, not a web tool or third-party helpdesk.
    • Production scale: high-volume traffic across a 40M-user app from day one, so it had to be multi-tenant, load-balanced and autoscaled.
    • Data stays controlled: subscriber data sits in a secure layer, and phone number + OTP verification comes before any account data.
    • Guides, never overrides: it can raise complaints and escalate, but it never activates packages or changes plans on its own.
    The bottom lineSecurity, scale, personalisation and action boundaries all had to hold at the same time. They shaped every architectural decision.

    What we delivered
    • Built and deployed the assistant: a customer-facing AI assistant inside the operator's app, live in production.
    • Connected it to the operator's systems: internal customer data for account answers, and approved SOPs, policies and documents through Amazon Kendra.
    • Secured access: phone-number and OTP verification before any account data is accessed.
    • Built a sandboxed LLM layer: AWS Bedrock with a locally hosted Llama model on EC2, keeping subscriber data in a controlled environment.
    • Designed it for production scale: multi-tenant, load-balanced and autoscaled on AWS, with DynamoDB for session and memory.
    • Set the action limits: confirmation before customer-impacting actions, and clean escalation when the assistant can't resolve a query.

    Sandboxed by design

    Subscriber data never leaves a controlled layer

    Llama runs locally on EC2. Knowledge answers come only from the operator's approved SOPs.

    What's happening underneath
    • Verify first: phone number + OTP confirms the customer before any account data is accessed.
    • Account questions take the private path: load-balanced and autoscaled. Llama, hosted locally on EC2, handles the verified customer's account data inside the sandbox; AWS Bedrock is the reasoning layer that composes the reply.
    • Knowledge questions take the approved path: Amazon Kendra retrieves from the operator's SOPs and policies, and Bedrock writes a grounded answer.
    • Memory across the chat: DynamoDB keeps session context, so follow-up questions just work.
    • Clear action boundaries: anything customer-impacting needs the customer to confirm; unresolved cases escalate cleanly to a person.
    • Every reply is a safe answer: grounded in the customer's data or the operator's approved knowledge, never open-ended model output.
