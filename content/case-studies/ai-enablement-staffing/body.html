    In a nutshell

    The firm saw that AI could transform recruitment, client service and operations, but giving people access to the tools did not produce real gains. Prompting was inconsistent, use cases were not standardised and AI sat outside daily work. We ran a combined enablement and automation programme: structured training, AI-assisted workflows, integrations with the systems teams already use, coordination of the firm's external AI platform partner, and AI-assisted candidate screening.

    Client data protectedThe client is anonymised, and every candidate, client, message and timing in the demos is fictional. The workflows are real; the data is not.
    01 · The problem

    Access is not adoption

    Everyone could open an AI tool. Almost nobody used one the same way twice, and none of it was part of a standard workflow. Compare how AI was used across the firm before and after the programme.

    Not usedAd hoc, each person their own wayStandard AI workflow
    Illustrative picture of adoption, based on the challenges and outcomes described.
    What was going wrong
    • Inconsistent usage: every team, and every person, prompted differently.
    • Low-value use: high-value AI workflows were rarely adopted.
    • Fragmented tools: AI lived in separate windows, outside Slack, Outlook and Egnyte.
    • Admin overload: significant time went on repetitive emails, formatting and scheduling.
    • No one coordinating: internal teams and the external AI platform partner were not aligned.
    • No measurement: nobody could say what AI was actually worth to the firm.
