    01 · The problem

    Eighty engineers. Ideas everywhere. No single view.

    The hub wanted engineers to own their AI projects. That left leadership unable to answer four basic questions. Tap one to see the gap.

    The client's innovation hub
    About 80 engineers across seven business areas · positions and counts are sanitised
    What was missing
    • Visibility: no single place captured every AI idea, so leadership could not see what was built, on which platform, or by whom.
    • A support path: a stuck engineer had no defined way to ask for help, and nobody could spot them.
    • Sensitive ideas: some touch confidential R&D, customer or export-controlled material and needed review without slowing everyone else.
    • Ownership kept: the answer could not be a central team building tools and pushing them down.
    02 · The rules

    The sponsor set the rules before any design

    Every later decision follows from six rules. Tap a rule to see what it ruled out and what we did instead.

    The success testAn engineer must never experience the process as bureaucracy.
    Registering an idea is not authorising it
    • A record, not a permission: registering makes an idea visible and lets the engineer ask for support. It does not authorise the work.
    • Stages track progress: Triaged, Approved and the later stages show where an idea stands. An engineer does not wait for any of them before starting to build.
    • Review where it is needed: ideas that may touch confidential, R&D, customer or export-controlled material are flagged at intake for the governance owner to review.
    • The minimum layer: it is not an enterprise AI governance suite. It adds intake, tracking, support and reporting inside the client's existing security boundaries, licensing and administration.

      Why intake is built this way
      • Nine questions, mostly hidden: four core questions plus identity; the "other tool" and "what kind of help" questions appear only when they apply.
      • A standalone form on purpose: a list-bound form would skip the flow, losing the identity lookup, the sensitivity check, the messages and the failure alert.
      • Ownership in one variable: the governance owner is read from one setting, so it can change without rebuilding any step.
      • Never lost silently: a parallel branch alerts the governance owner if the SharePoint write fails.

        • A board that writes back: Power BI is normally read-only, so a dropped card goes to an Azure Function that rejects unknown IDs and any status outside the seven.

          How a sensitive idea is handled, in order
          • 1 · The engineer's answer sets the flag: any answer other than No, including Not sure, sets it. Over-flagging is the safer error.
          • 2 · The intake flow raises it: the governance alert starts with [SENSITIVE DATA REVIEW] and the idea lands in a dedicated Sensitive Review view for the governance owner.
          • 3 · The governance owner restricts it when needed: item-level permissions limit the record to governance owners and the assigned support contact. The wider engineering community can no longer see it.
          • 4 · Same register: sensitive ideas stay in the one list, so reporting and the lifecycle work the same way.

            What leadership gets
            • At-a-glance counts: total ideas, and those needing triage, needing support, in build, deployed and under sensitive review.
            • A built-in check: with no status filter, the seven stage counts add up to the total; that is how the team checks the numbers.
            • A live activity feed: status changes, Teams messages, new submissions, flags and support assignments, with who or what did each.
            • An inventory of shipped AI: deployed ideas form a running list of tools the hub can show to the wider business.
            Register and dashboard: who uses which
            • The register is the record: every idea, owner, stage, support need and triage note lives in the AI Idea Registry SharePoint list, with version history.
            • The dashboard is a read-out: Power BI counts, charts and the board are built over the register. A card move is written back to the register.
            • Engineers: submit in Microsoft Forms and hear back in Teams. They can view the dashboard, views and board, but cannot edit items or move cards.
            • Governance owners: manage the portfolio. They triage, set priorities, assign support and set permissions on register items, and move cards on the board.

            Built to be owned by the client
            • Three flows and one function: intake, status-change notification and board write-back, plus the UpdateIdeaStatus function in the client's Azure subscription.
            • Standard licensing: intake, register and notifications run on E3/E5, with no external SaaS, no Dataverse and no Copilot Studio dependency.
            • Details that matter: column names and choice values match the form character for character, or SharePoint rejects the write.
            • Handed over in full: a technical design document and a rebuild guide with a check at every step, so the hub runs it without Paramount.
